**Effective date: 28 September 2026**

This policy explains what happens to information when you play **Fomalhaut
Raiders** ("the game"), a mobile space shooter published by Muhammad Hazki
Hariowibowo, trading as MHz Games ("we", "us").

---

## The short version

- The game has **no accounts, no login and no servers of its own**, and never
  asks who you are.
- The game sends us a **small amount of anonymous usage data**: how far
  you have got, which sorties were flown and how they went, how the flight
  school tutorial went, and what was built or upgraded in the hangar. It is
  sent under a random ID that is not linked to you.
- This version has **no ads and no in-app purchases**. Future versions will
  add them, and this policy will be updated before they are released.
- Your progress (credits, crystals, parts, fitted mods and settings) is
  saved **only on your own device**. Your device's own cloud backup may include
  it if you use one. We can't see it either way.

## 1. Information the game collects

**Nothing that identifies you.** The game has no accounts and never asks for
your name, email address, contacts, location or anything similar. It does not
read your contacts, location, photos, microphone, camera, advertising ID or any
other identifier on your device.

### Usage data

To see where the game is too hard or too easy, it sends us a small number of
**anonymous usage events**:

- **The app was opened**, and whether this was the first time.
- **How far you have got**, once each time you open the game: the deepest
  ring reached, how many finales you have won, how many sorties you have flown
  and how many ended in a dock, your best kill count, your crystal and credit
  balances, how many blueprints and tuners you hold, and whether you have
  finished flight school. This is the same progress the game already shows you.
- **Flight school**: when it starts, each lesson passed and how long it took,
  and how it ended (docked, crashed or skipped).
- **Sorties**: when one starts, which ring, sector, mission, tier and threat
  level it is and which ship parts you are flying; when it ends, how (docked,
  destroyed, failed or abandoned), how long it lasted, how many enemies you
  destroyed, the credits and crystals it paid, and how many parts and mods were
  kept or lost.
- **The hangar**: what was built, upgraded, salvaged, reclaimed, merged or
  fused, and how many crystals and credits it cost or returned.

Credits and crystals are in-game currency only. They are not money and cannot
be cashed out.

Each event also carries the app version and build number, the operating
system, a **random installation ID** that the game generates the first time it
runs, when that ID was created, and a second random ID for the current run of
the app. The installation ID is not derived from your device, your
advertising identifier or anything about you, and we cannot connect it to you.
Uninstalling the game discards it.

This data is received and stored for us by **PostHog**, a product-analytics
service, on servers in the United States. Like any web service, PostHog sees
the IP address the events arrive from. The game tells PostHog not to store it
and not to work out a location from it, and our PostHog project is set to
discard it. PostHog processes this data only on our behalf, under its own
privacy policy: <https://posthog.com/privacy>.

The usage data is sent in every country. It is anonymous and is not used for
advertising, and it is never shared with anyone else.

### Data kept on your device

The game also writes files to your device's private app storage:

| What | Why |
|---|---|
| Credits, crystals, unlocked parts, fitted mods and settings | So you keep what you earned, and the game opens the way you left it |
| The random installation ID described above | So the usage data from one install is counted once |

These files are readable only by the game. **We never receive them and have
no way to access them**; the game does not upload them anywhere. Android's
own backup may copy them to your Google account if you have backup turned on.
That copy is handled by Google under your agreement with Google, and we can't
see it.

Uninstalling the game or clearing its storage deletes these files.

## 2. Advertising

This version shows no ads and contains no advertising SDK.

A future version will show ads supplied by a third-party ad network, which will
receive information from your device in order to serve them. The network, what
it receives and the choices you have about it will be set out here before that
version is released.

## 3. Purchases

This version has no in-app purchases.

A future version will offer in-app purchases. They will be processed by Google
Play, and we will never see or store your payment details. Details will be
added here before that version is released.

## 4. Google Play

You download the game from Google Play, and Google processes information
about that download (and any crash reports your device chooses to send
through Android) under its own privacy policy:
<https://policies.google.com/privacy>. We may see those crash reports in
aggregate, with no information that identifies you, and use them only to fix
the game.

## 5. Children

The game is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect
personal information from children under 13. The usage data described in
section 1 is anonymous for every player, children included. If you believe a
child has provided personal information through the game, contact us at the
address below and we will act on it.

## 6. Data retention

Usage data (section 1) is kept for as long as it is useful to us, and for **at
least twelve months**, the minimum PostHog holds it for on the plan we use. We
remove it when we no longer need it, and all of it goes when we close the
PostHog project. We never build profiles of individual players from it.
Data stored on your device stays there until you uninstall the game or clear
its storage.

## 7. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete or
restrict the processing of your personal data, or to object to it.

The usage data we hold is not linked to you. It sits under a random
installation ID that the game keeps in its own private storage and never
displays, so there is nothing you could send us that would identify your
events, and nothing we could use to find them. That is why the data cannot be
traced back to you, and it is also why we cannot pick your events out of it
to delete them on request.

**To stop usage data being sent**, uninstall the game. There is no separate
switch for it inside the game yet. Uninstalling also discards the installation
ID, so nothing sent after a reinstall can be connected to what came before.
For anything else, contact us and we will help.

## 8. Changes to this policy

Ads and in-app purchases are planned for future versions. When a version adds
them, or changes what usage data is sent, this policy will be updated before
that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
